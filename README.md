

# <span style="color:#336699;">Introduction</span>

Hi, I'm Genevieve Comer-Kleine!

When I was asked in primary school what I wanted to be when I grew up, I said "a cancer researcher".
Years later, I'm working towards that goal as a  Master of Bioinformatics student at the University of Melbourne, using long-read RNA sequencing data to study melanoma. 

My path here has taken me through a Bachelor of Data Science and 1.5 years working at a SaaS company using machine learning to guide product decisions. Now, I'm bringing that computational background to cancer research. 

## <span style="color:#990000;">Fields of Interest</span>

### <span style="color:#009900;">Deep Learning and Neural Networks</span>

One of my areas of interest is deep learning and neural networks. During my undergraduate degree in data science, I developed a convolutional neural network (CNN) to classify cancerous cells from a dataset of colon histopathology images. It was my first experience with deep learning, and I found it fascinating. I then took this project a step further by automating the detection and extraction of individual cells from higher-resolution histology images. This project taught me a lot about applications of deep learning in the medical field and got me really interested in how computational methods can be applied to medical sciences. 

### <span style="color:#009900;">Data Visualization and Visual Analytics</span>

Like most data enthusiasts I appreciate the importance of accurately telling the stories in my data. Data visualisations allows for me to draw on my creative side and is a great pleasure to do, but its not just about making something pretty, it's important to make sure stories in the data are being accurately told. This require careful consideration and often is a collaborative process. This part of working with data is often not a significant concern in most pipelines, but I believe how we represent our data is one of the most crucial aspects of any research and should be done with significant care. 

## <span style="color:#009900;">My stint in the tech world</span>

When I applied for data science I know it was an avenue into medical research, but it was important for me to really understand the broad landscape of the industry. Before starting my masters I took a gap year as a Data Scientist Intern and then full time employee at a Mid-size software company. During my time in this role I was the primary data scientist in the company working in a team of data engineers. As a result I designed and implemented several key data initiative, primarily focused on customer behavior insights using machine learning. I was able to create an interactive customer health program which was driven by data insights, as well as a customer survival projection metric, which utilised customer data and behavioral patterns to predict health and lifetime value. 

This opportunity allowed me to dip my toes into the development world, and build skills in designing projects, from stakeholder wishes. It also taught me how to communicate my work to non technical stakeholders and colleagues.
